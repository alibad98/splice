# PRD Quality Review — Slips (سليبس) MVP PRD v0.1 (`docs/prd.md`)

## Overall verdict
The PRD gets the sequencing and most of the detail right. Epic 1 is a real de-risking spike: it puts the Salla install → inject → add-to-cart → order-webhook loop ahead of anything else. Most stories have testable ACs, and the performance and privacy NFRs come with real thresholds. What is at risk is the thesis itself. The PRD bets everything on "the app proves its own ROI", but it never defines what an attributed order is (click vs view, gross vs net of cancellations). It also has no success metrics or launch exit criteria, and it quietly drops roughly a third of the SRS's MVP non-functional baseline while still naming the SRS as "source of truth for detail". For a launch-stakes PRD, it is not ready to green-light build until it settles attribution semantics, carries over the SRS exit criteria, makes an honest scope reconciliation against SRS §10.1, and puts owners and deadlines on the launch-blocking open questions (Q-04 pricing, Q-05 residency).

## Decision-readiness — thin

The PRD makes some real decisions and states them as decisions: "use a managed video service (for example Mux or Cloudflare Stream) rather than a custom transcoding pipeline" (§4), "last-touch, default 7-day window" (FR13), and "deactivate-not-delete on downgrade" (FR19). Story 1.6 AC3 ("any change to FR13 is flagged to the PM") is a good conditional decision hook. But none of these decisions says what was given up. Last-touch vs multi-touch, click-through vs view-through, and the 7-day window are exactly where a merchant or Salla reviewer will push back (SRS R-02 "Attribution accuracy disputes"), and the PRD gives no rationale.

The bigger problem is §7 "Open Items". It lists six questions with no owner, no deadline and no statement of what each one blocks. Q-04 "Final plan names and prices" blocks Story 7.2 and the Salla listing (Story 7.6). Q-05 "Data residency" blocks hosting and PDPL sign-off (Story 7.5). Both are launch-blocking, yet they sit at the same weight as Q-02 and Q-03, which are Phase 2 questions. Q-06 "Social import timing" is not actually open: §5 already places social import in Epic 9 (Phase 3). The PRD has no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at all, even where real tensions exist (see findings).

### Findings
- **high** Launch-blocking open questions have no owner, deadline or "blocks" link (§7, Q-04, Q-05) — Q-04 gates FR19/Story 7.2 plan limits ("content and placement limits") and the listing in Story 7.6, and Q-05 gates §4 "Hosting" and Story 7.5. As written, nobody can tell whether these must close before Epic 7 starts. *Fix:* For each Q, add an owner, a needed-by epic or story, and a default to assume if it is unresolved (e.g. "KSA region unless counsel says otherwise").
- **high** No contingency if the Salla spike fails (§5 Epic 1, Story 1.5/1.6, Q-01) — The whole product depends on script injection, cart add from injected script, and order-to-session matching. Story 1.6 AC3 only says changes are "flagged to the PM". There is no stated fallback (e.g. coupon-code attribution, UTM/landing-param attribution, an App Snippet vs a manual theme paste) and no go/no-go criterion for the spike. *Fix:* Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` with explicit spike exit criteria and the pre-agreed fallback for each of injection, cart action and attribution.
- **high** Injector installation model undecided but presented as settled (FR17 "installation guide", FR18 "installation instructions", Story 5.1 "one script to enable Slips", Story 7.1 AC2) — Several screens imply a manual merchant install step. Whether Salla injects automatically or the merchant pastes a snippet decides onboarding friction, support load and activation rate, and it is never stated as a decision. *Fix:* State the intended model (or both, with the spike choosing), and say what the merchant has to do.
- **medium** Q-06 is rhetorical (§7 item 6 vs §5 Epic 9 "social import") — It is already answered by the phasing. *Fix:* Remove it or mark it resolved.
- **medium** SRS open questions silently dropped (§7) — SRS Q-07 (multi-platform, "affects architecture"), Q-08 (non-video slides in v1), Q-09 (content moderation/abuse) and Q-10 (support model/SLAs) do not appear. Q-07 and Q-09 matter to the Architect and to Salla review. *Fix:* Carry them over or mark them explicitly as closed or deferred.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Most of the PRD is load-bearing. The Goals (§1) are product-specific ("≤ 50 KB initial, ~20 KB core", "attributed orders and revenue"), the NFRs mostly have thresholds (NFR1, NFR2, NFR7), and the PRD avoids persona padding by having no personas at all. The theater is concentrated in two places. The differentiation paragraph (§1 Background) claims "Slips differentiates with Stories + Reels, scheduling and visibility rules, order-level attribution, and (later) AI…" against Na3na3, but gives no evidence that the competitor lacks attribution or scheduling, and half the claimed differentiation ("later AI") is out of MVP scope. §3 UI Design Goals opens with an adjective list: "clean, modern, merchant-friendly dashboard".

### Findings
- **medium** Differentiation claim unverified and partly out of scope (§1 Background) — "An existing competitor (Na3na3) offers shoppable video only." The MVP's real wedge vs the competitor is not stated in testable terms: is it attribution accuracy, player weight, or Stories format? *Fix:* Name the one or two MVP-deliverable differentiators, with evidence (e.g. a competitor's player size or attribution method), and drop "(later) AI" from the MVP pitch.
- **low** Adjective UI goals (§3 "clean, modern, merchant-friendly") — These do no work. The demo reference (`slips-sage.vercel.app`) is the real spec. *Fix:* Replace them with "match the demo, with the deviations listed in SRS Appendix B".
- **low** Goal with no bound (§1 "Reach a pilot-ready MVP quickly, then fund Phases 2-3 from pilot revenue") — "quickly" has no date, and "fund from pilot revenue" has no pricing (Q-04 open) or revenue target. *Fix:* Give a target date and a pilot revenue/merchant-count target, or remove the funding claim.

## Strategic coherence — thin

There is a clear thesis: a lightweight, shoppable Stories/Reels player whose attribution proves ROI, so merchants keep paying. Epic ordering follows it well. Epic 1 de-risks the loop, Epic 5 has the shopper purchase path, and Epic 6 closes the ROI loop. The MVP is a problem-solving plus revenue MVP, and the scope mostly matches. The deferrals to Epic 8/9 (AI, audience, roles) are thesis-consistent.

However, the PRD has no Success Metrics section and no counter-metrics. The SRS §10.1 already has concrete MVP exit criteria: "reconcile with Salla order data within an agreed tolerance (for example ±3%)", "At least [TBD, for example 10] pilot merchants live, each with published content and at least one attributed order", "zero measured layout shift on at least three representative themes". The PRD reduces this to Story 7.6 AC2 "within agreed tolerance". Nothing measures whether merchants actually see ROI (attributed revenue per store, retention past trial, publish rate), and nothing guards against the obvious harm. The counter-metric should be the store's Core Web Vitals (SRS NFR-PERF-07, dropped) or attribution disputes.

### Findings
- **critical** No Success Metrics or launch exit criteria (whole PRD; Story 7.6 AC2 "within agreed tolerance") — A launch-stakes PRD whose thesis is "the app proves its own ROI" has no measure of whether the thesis held and no definition of "ready to launch". *Fix:* Add an SM section. Import the SRS §10.1 exit criteria with numbers (±3% reconciliation, N pilots, 3 themes, 0 open critical/high). Add thesis metrics (e.g. % of installs that publish content within 7 days, % of active stores with ≥1 attributed order in 30 days, trial-to-paid). Add counter-metrics (store CWV delta vs baseline, attribution-dispute support tickets).
- **high** The attribution definition, which is the core of the thesis, is underspecified (FR13, Story 6.2 AC1–AC2, Story 1.6) — FR13 says "last-touch, default 7-day window, configurable". Story 6.2 says "matched to tagged sessions or clicks". It is unclear whether a view without a click counts, what "touch" means, whether cancelled or refunded orders reduce attributed revenue, whether revenue is gross or net of VAT/shipping, and who configures the window and within what range. Story 6.2 AC2 lists edge cases ("multiple touches, expired window, cancelled orders") without expected outcomes. *Fix:* Add an attribution spec block: qualifying touch types, window bounds (e.g. 1–30 days, merchant-configurable), the revenue basis, and the expected result for each listed edge case.
- **medium** "Fund Phases 2-3 from pilot revenue" has no revenue mechanics in MVP (§1 Goals vs Q-04, FR19) — The revenue goal is asserted, but plan limits and prices are open. Story 7.2 AC4 "Trial period works if configured" leaves even the trial undecided. *Fix:* Either state the MVP pricing hypothesis or demote the goal to "validate willingness to pay".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the strongest part of the PRD. Most stories carry specific, testable ACs: Story 1.2 AC1 "CI fails if the player's gzip size exceeds 50 KB", Story 2.2 AC1 "within one minute", Story 4.2 AC1 "in the store's time zone", Story 7.1 AC2 "regenerate (old key invalidated)", Story 6.3 AC1 with named date ranges. The weaker spots are the places where the PRD defers detail to the SRS or to "a check" that is never defined, and several of those land on high-traffic or launch-critical paths.

### Findings
- **high** Undefined thresholds on upload, processing and quality-check paths (FR5, Story 3.3 AC1, Story 3.4 AC2) — "unsupported type/size/duration is rejected" and "run a quality check" name no limits or criteria. SRS NFR-PERF-06 ("60-second video … within 5 minutes at P95") was dropped too. *Fix:* List the accepted formats, max size, max duration, the quality-check criteria (resolution/aspect/duration/bitrate) and a processing-time bound.
- **medium** Behavior when multiple items match a placement is undefined (FR8, FR9, Story 4.3 AC1) — SRS FR-VIS-06 (priority ordering) is deferred, but MVP still needs a rule (e.g. most-recent-first, cap N). *Fix:* State the MVP ordering rule and the per-placement cap.
- **medium** Layout selection has no home (Story 5.5 AC2 "Layout is selected per placement") — Neither FR8 nor Story 4.1 includes a layout field. The relationship between content "format" (Story/Reels, FR4) and layout (strip/carousel/full-screen) is also unstated. *Fix:* Add layout to the placement rule in FR8/Story 4.1 and give the format × layout matrix.
- **medium** Deleted-product handling points at an FR that doesn't define it (Story 2.2 AC2 "handled per FR16") — FR16 covers out-of-stock only ("CTA disabled, 'Out of stock'"), not deletion. *Fix:* Define it: content auto-unpublished, or the CTA hidden, plus a merchant notice.
- **medium** Vague bounds in places (FR10 "fails gracefully"; Story 2.1 AC3 "clear error and are retried"; Story 5.3 AC2 "based on network quality"; Story 1.2 AC2 "any measurable shift"; Story 5.2 AC2 "iOS Safari and Android Chrome" with no versions; NFR2 "typical 4G"; Story 7.2 AC3 "grace period" with no length; Story 7.5 AC2 "retention rules implemented" with no rules) — Each needs a bound, and most already exist in the SRS (NFR-COMP-02 versions, NFR-DATA-01 30 days, FR-INJ-09). *Fix:* Inline the numbers.
- **low** Metric definitions arrive after the screens that use them (Story 6.5 after 6.3/6.4; FR15 "four KPIs" unnamed) — Analytics and Overview stories will be built before "View", "CTR" and "Engagement" are defined. *Fix:* Move the glossary into the PRD (or make 6.5 a prerequisite of 6.3) and name the four Overview KPIs.

## Scope honesty — thin

On the surface, scope looks honest. MVP vs Phase 2/3 is explicit (§5 "Later phases"), and "Audience arrives in Phase 2" (§3) is called out. Underneath, the PRD declares the SRS "source of truth for detail" but then drops SRS items silently. SRS §10.1 says the MVP includes "Non-functional: All of Section 5", yet NFR1–NFR8 leave out:
- performance: NFR-PERF-06, NFR-PERF-07 (CWV impact)
- scale: NFR-SCAL-01 (10× seasonal peaks: Ramadan, White Friday)
- compatibility: all of 5.6 (theme matrix, browser versions, Shadow DOM isolation)
- cost and abuse: all of 5.8
- data: NFR-DATA-02/03 (analytics retention, merchant export/deletion), a PDPL obligation
- operations: NFR-OBS-02 (internal admin console), NFR-REL-01

The SRS MVP functional set also includes FR-CON-03, FR-CON-13, FR-CON-17 and FR-NOT-03 ("Need help? Contact support" on every page). None is cited, and FR-NOT-03 has no story. Nothing tells the reader whether these were de-scoped or overlooked.

Open-items density is low (6 Qs, 1 `[Assumption]`, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, no Non-Goals section). That is low because tensions are unmarked, not because they are resolved. The SRS's own `[Assumption]` tags (30-day retention, content statuses, stock behavior) lose their tags when they pass into the PRD.

### Findings
- **critical** Silent NFR de-scoping contradicts the SRS phasing rule (§2 Non-Functional vs SRS §10.0 rule 2 / §10.1 "All of Section 5") — The 10× seasonal peak, browser and theme compatibility, the admin console and merchant data export/deletion are all launch-relevant for Salla review and PDPL. Because §1 header calls the SRS "source of truth", a downstream reader cannot tell which document wins. *Fix:* Either restore them as NFR9+ with thresholds, or list them in a Non-Goals/Deferred table with a reason. Also state the precedence rule between the PRD and the SRS.
- **high** SRS MVP functional requirements dropped without a trace (FR4–FR7 citations; no story for FR-NOT-03) — FR-CON-03 (statuses including Archived/Processing/Failed), FR-CON-13 (CTA action types: add to cart / open product / open link), FR-CON-17 and FR-NOT-03 are in SRS MVP scope. *Fix:* Cite them or explicitly de-scope them, and add a support-entry story to Epic 7.
- **medium** Contradiction: RBAC cited in MVP while roles are deferred (NFR3 "[NFR-SEC-01 to 07]" includes NFR-SEC-04 RBAC; §5 Epic 8 "team roles") — *Fix:* Exclude SEC-04 from NFR3, or state "single owner account in MVP" as a non-goal.
- **medium** Contradiction: theme matching (§3 "storefront player inherits basic store theme colors" vs SRS FR-INJ-08, deferred to Phase 2) — *Fix:* Mark it as a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` or bring a minimal version into scope with a story.
- **medium** No Non-Goals section or Assumptions index — Plausible silent assumptions are not ruled out, e.g. one merchant user per store, single currency (SAR), no content moderation (SRS Q-09), no multi-product per content (FR-BUY-03 deferred). *Fix:* Add a short Non-Goals list, and an Assumptions index that restores the SRS `[Assumption]` tags carried into FR2, FR6 and FR16.
- **low** SRS Appendix B items 3–5 not addressed (Story 6.5 AC2 resolves "items 1 and 2" only) — These cover the out-of-stock guidance, step-to-step funnel rates, and the undesigned Visibility rules screen. *Fix:* Assign each one or mark it deferred.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD is chain-top: §8 names the Architect, UX and PO agents. On the plus side, IDs are contiguous (FR1–FR19, NFR1–NFR8, Stories 1.1–7.6), and every FR carries SRS trace IDs, which helps extraction a lot. Epics map cleanly to FRs. The problems are a missing glossary (deferred to Story 6.5 even though downstream agents need it first) and noticeable term drift on core nouns. The PRD also leans on the SRS without saying which document wins. A UX agent extracting §3 gets a screen list but no journeys or states, and the "Visibility rules" screen is a known gap (SRS Appendix B item 5).

### Findings
- **high** No glossary while core terms drift (whole PRD; Story 6.5 AC1 promises one) — The term pairs in Mechanical notes ("placement" vs "visibility rules", "injector" vs "player" vs "loader" vs "script", status sets) will produce inconsistent data models and UI copy downstream. *Fix:* Add a Glossary section before §2 that defines Content item, Story, Reel, Format, Layout, Placement, Visibility rule, Injector, Player, Injector key, View, Unique viewer, CTR, Engagement, Completion, Touch, Attributed order and Attributed revenue.
- **medium** PRD/SRS precedence undefined (header "Source of truth for detail: `docs/Slips_SRS_v0.1.md`") — When the PRD omits or alters SRS content (see Scope honesty), downstream agents get conflicting instructions. *Fix:* State that "the PRD governs MVP scope and the SRS governs detail for cited IDs only", or the reverse.
- **low** Sections lean on cross-refs that don't resolve standalone (Story 4.3 AC3 "follows FR16", Story 2.2 AC2 "per FR16", Story 5.4 AC3 "per Story 1.6") — Story 1.6's output is a doc that doesn't exist yet. *Fix:* Acceptable for now. Once spike findings land, inline the tagging mechanism in Story 5.4.

## Shape fit — thin

The document uses the classic BMAD v4 PRD shape (Goals → FR/NFR → UI goals → Tech assumptions → Epics and Stories). It fits story generation well. But this is a multi-stakeholder B2B2C product with three distinct experiences: the merchant dashboard, the shopper storefront player, and the Salla platform/reviewer. The SRS §2.3 already identifies five user classes (Merchant Owner, Content Staff, Analyst, Shopper, Slips Admin). The PRD has no user journeys and no named protagonists. Stories use a mix of "merchant", "shopper", "developer", "the team", "the system", "the player", "the business" and "product owner". The end-to-end activation journey is exactly where the product will win or lose, and it exists only implicitly across six epics: install → product sync → injector live → first content → first add-to-cart → first attributed order. The Slips Admin class (support console, abuse) disappears entirely. The PRD is over-formalized on implementation (§4 stack candidates, Story 1.1 monorepo ACs) and under-formalized on user experience.

### Findings
- **high** No user journeys for a multi-stakeholder consumer-facing product (whole PRD; §3 lists screens only) — The UX agent (§8) has nothing to build the onboarding flow, the empty states or the shopper's watch → card → cart → continue flow from. *Fix:* Add 3–4 UJs with named protagonists. Suggested: Merchant Owner first-week activation (install to first attributed order), Content Staff creating and scheduling a campaign, a Shopper on mobile product page through add-to-cart, and Merchant Owner disputing or verifying attributed revenue.
- **medium** Internal operator role dropped (SRS user class "Slips Admin"; NFR-OBS-02 admin console) — Launch support and pilot onboarding (Story 7.6) need a way to look up store sync and job state. *Fix:* Add an operator capability requirement or declare it a non-goal with a workaround (e.g. direct DB access for the pilot).
