Slips PRD v0.1 — Validation Report

docs/prd.md
Poor

The PRD gets the sequencing and most of the detail right. Epic 1 is a real de-risking spike: it puts the Salla install → inject → add-to-cart → order-webhook loop ahead of anything else. Most stories have testable ACs, and the performance and privacy NFRs come with real thresholds. What is at risk is the thesis itself. The PRD bets everything on "the app proves its own ROI", but it never defines what an attributed order is (click vs view, gross vs net of cancellations). It also has no success metrics or launch exit criteria, and it quietly drops roughly a third of the SRS's MVP non-functional baseline while still naming the SRS as "source of truth for detail". For a launch-stakes PRD, it is not ready to green-light build until it settles attribution semantics, carries over the SRS exit criteria, makes an honest scope reconciliation against SRS §10.1, and puts owners and deadlines on the launch-blocking open questions (Q-04 pricing, Q-05 residency).

The two extra reviewers make the picture worse, not different. The adversarial review raises the bar from "not ready to green-light" to "not ready to hand to UX, architecture, or story sharding": it adds critical defects the rubric did not surface — add-to-cart ships without product variants, "zero layout shift" cannot coexist with async, gracefully-failing rendering, PDPL is one bullet while order webhooks bring shopper PII into the attribution store, and the injector key is a public identifier dressed up as a secret, which also makes event ingestion spoofable. The SRS/stack consistency review confirms the silent de-scoping item by item (FR-NOT-03, NFR-OBS-02 admin console, NFR-COMP, NFR-DATA, NFR-COST, PERF-06/07, SCAL-01) and shows that PRD §4 is stale against docs/tech-stack.md, which leaves several architecture-shaping needs (CLS-safe Web Component, CDN purge vs scheduling, analytics partitioning, tenant isolation) with no home. After deduplication the three reviews come to 66 distinct findings: 8 critical, 20 high, 26 medium, 12 low (see validation-report.md).

Decision-readiness

thin

The PRD makes some real decisions and states them as decisions: "use a managed video service (for example Mux or Cloudflare Stream) rather than a custom transcoding pipeline" (§4), "last-touch, default 7-day window" (FR13), and "deactivate-not-delete on downgrade" (FR19). Story 1.6 AC3 ("any change to FR13 is flagged to the PM") is a good conditional decision hook. But none of these decisions says what was given up. Last-touch vs multi-touch, click-through vs view-through, and the 7-day window are exactly where a merchant or Salla reviewer will push back (SRS R-02 "Attribution accuracy disputes"), and the PRD gives no rationale.

The bigger problem is §7 "Open Items". It lists six questions with no owner, no deadline and no statement of what each one blocks. Q-04 "Final plan names and prices" blocks Story 7.2 and the Salla listing (Story 7.6). Q-05 "Data residency" blocks hosting and PDPL sign-off (Story 7.5). Both are launch-blocking, yet they sit at the same weight as Q-02 and Q-03, which are Phase 2 questions. Q-06 "Social import timing" is not actually open: §5 already places social import in Epic 9 (Phase 3). The PRD has no [NOTE FOR PM] callouts at all, even where real tensions exist.

Strategic coherence

thin

There is a clear thesis: a lightweight, shoppable Stories/Reels player whose attribution proves ROI, so merchants keep paying. Epic ordering follows it well. Epic 1 de-risks the loop, Epic 5 has the shopper purchase path, and Epic 6 closes the ROI loop. The MVP is a problem-solving plus revenue MVP, and the scope mostly matches. The deferrals to Epic 8/9 (AI, audience, roles) are thesis-consistent.

However, the PRD has no Success Metrics section and no counter-metrics. The SRS §10.1 already has concrete MVP exit criteria: "reconcile with Salla order data within an agreed tolerance (for example ±3%)", "At least [TBD, for example 10] pilot merchants live, each with published content and at least one attributed order", "zero measured layout shift on at least three representative themes". The PRD reduces this to Story 7.6 AC2 "within agreed tolerance". Nothing measures whether merchants actually see ROI (attributed revenue per store, retention past trial, publish rate), and nothing guards against the obvious harm. The counter-metric should be the store's Core Web Vitals (SRS NFR-PERF-07, dropped) or attribution disputes.

Shape fit

thin

The document uses the classic BMAD v4 PRD shape (Goals → FR/NFR → UI goals → Tech assumptions → Epics and Stories). It fits story generation well. But this is a multi-stakeholder B2B2C product with three distinct experiences: the merchant dashboard, the shopper storefront player, and the Salla platform/reviewer. The SRS §2.3 already identifies five user classes (Merchant Owner, Content Staff, Analyst, Shopper, Slips Admin). The PRD has no user journeys and no named protagonists. Stories use a mix of "merchant", "shopper", "developer", "the team", "the system", "the player", "the business" and "product owner".

The end-to-end activation journey is exactly where the product will win or lose, and it exists only implicitly across six epics: install → product sync → injector live → first content → first add-to-cart → first attributed order. The Slips Admin class (support console, abuse) disappears entirely. The PRD is over-formalized on implementation (§4 stack candidates, Story 1.1 monorepo ACs) and under-formalized on user experience.

Adversarial review

review-adversarial-general.md

This PRD is not ready to hand to UX, architecture, or story sharding. It is a compressed index of the SRS that keeps the feature list but drops the parts that make a launch survivable: it has no success metrics or counter-metrics, it builds seven epics on Salla capabilities that are explicitly unverified while giving the spike no go/no-go gate, and its central value claim ("attributed orders and revenue, so the app proves its own ROI") rests on an attribution definition that is under-specified, self-inflating, and spoofable through a public injector key with bot filtering deferred to Phase 2.

Several requirements contradict each other in ways that will surface as soon as someone designs or builds them: zero layout shift vs. graceful failure, WCAG 2.1 AA vs. deferred captions, add-to-cart in MVP vs. variants in Phase 2, and a "secret" injector key that has to sit in public storefront HTML. PDPL handling is reduced to one bullet while the app ingests order webhooks full of shopper PII and the data residency decision is still open. Fix the critical items before any downstream agent starts. Otherwise architecture will be designed around assumptions the spike may disprove, and the stories will pass their acceptance criteria while the product fails in the field.

Reviewer counts: Critical 7 · High 11 · Medium 11 · Low 6 · Total 35.

critical

C1. No success metrics, no counter-metrics, no definition of "launch"

§1 Goals; whole document; SRS §10.1 exit criteria · merged as C1
The goals cannot be tested. "Reach a pilot-ready MVP quickly", "so the app proves its own ROI" and "fund Phases 2-3 from pilot revenue" have no numbers, no owners and no time box. The SRS exit criteria are missing, so nothing defines "done" for the MVP. There are no counter-metrics, so the team can hit "attributed revenue up" while hurting store Core Web Vitals, store conversion, merchant trust or uninstall rate, and nobody will notice.
Fix: Add a Success metrics table (activation, weekly active merchants, pilot count and paid conversion, 30/60-day retention and uninstall rate, attributed revenue per store) and explicit counter-metrics (storefront LCP/INP/CLS delta vs baseline, add-to-cart error rate, attribution dispute rate, share of attributed revenue from orders with no Slips product). Copy the SRS exit criteria in and fill in every [TBD].
critical

C2. The whole MVP depends on unverified Salla capabilities, and the spike has no go/no-go gate

§4; Story 1.5; Story 1.6 AC3; §7 Q-01; §8 · merged as C2
The PRD admits that injection, cart actions, attribution tagging, webhooks and billing are assumptions, yet Epics 2-7 and FR9-FR14/FR19 are written as if every one of them works. Unverified: injection into every theme without merchant code edits; third-party add-to-cart incl. guest shoppers; a cart/order custom tag that the order webhook echoes back; metered/limit-based billing with trials; a storefront consent state. "Flagged to the PM" is a notification, not a decision.
Fix: Turn Epic 1 into a gate with written pass/fail criteria per capability and a named fallback for each failure (manual snippet / theme app block / no-go; SDK call / redirect / no-go; order note or thank-you beacon / demote FR13 to "assisted"). Epics 2-7 are not sharded until the gate passes; re-baseline §2 from docs/spike-findings.md.
critical

C3. In-content add to cart ships in the MVP, but product variants/options do not

FR11, Story 5.4 AC1-AC2; FR3; SRS §10.2 FR-PRD-07/08 · merged as C3
A large share of Salla catalogs (fashion, cosmetics shades, sizes) have required options. There is no option picker and sync doesn't import options or per-variant stock/price. "Tapping adds to the Salla cart" will fail, add a variant the shopper didn't pick, or show a mismatched price/stock. This is the core purchase flow, and it breaks for the very merchants most likely to use short-form video.
Fix: Either bring a minimal option selector and per-variant sync into the MVP, or downgrade the CTA to "View options" for products with required options and never call add-to-cart. Sync option/variant presence in FR3 and add a multi-option test fixture.
critical

C4. Attribution is under-defined and biased toward over-crediting Slips, so the ROI headline can be gamed

Goal 3; FR13; Story 6.2; Story 7.6 AC2; SRS FR-ANA-03, FR-ANA-12 · merged as C4
"Last-touch" means nothing when Slips sees only its own touches — it becomes "any Slips touch within 7 days", which claims credit for organic purchases. Whole-order vs line-item, shipping/VAT/discounts/refunds and competing content items are unaddressed. "Configurable" has no bounds: a sales demo can set 90 days and inflate the numbers. Bot filtering is deferred, so views, CTR and the funnel are inflated from day one. The Story 7.6 reconciliation is circular: Salla has no attribution ground truth.
Fix: Specify the model in the PRD. Count only orders containing a product added via Slips as "attributed"; report click-through as "assisted". Credit Slips line items only (ex shipping/VAT). Cap the window (e.g. 1-14 days) and show it on every revenue figure; reverse on cancel/refund; bring basic bot/duplicate filtering into MVP; define reconciliation as attributed order IDs existing in Salla with matching line items (≥ 99%) plus a manual audit; add a holdout experiment to Phase 2.
critical

C5. "Zero layout shift" contradicts "fails gracefully" and "renders only eligible content"

Goal 4; NFR1; FR9; FR10; Story 5.1 AC2; Story 5.5 AC1; Story 1.2 AC2 · merged as C5
Until the content API answers, the player can't know whether there is eligible content. Reserve space up front and you show an empty hole when there's no content, the API fails, or the key is invalid — a visible break that violates Story 5.1 AC2. Insert on response and you shift the layout, violating NFR1. Nothing in the PRD resolves this, and both UX and architecture need the answer on day one.
Fix: Decide the mechanism: server-side eligibility via injection if Salla allows it; non-shifting placements only (overlay, floating bubble, below the fold); or a merchant-placed fixed-height container. Rewrite the AC as "CLS contribution = 0.00 on N reference themes for: content present, no content, API timeout, invalid key".
critical

C6. PDPL is reduced to one bullet while the product ingests shopper PII and residency is undecided

NFR4; Story 6.1 AC2; Story 6.2; Story 7.5 AC2; §4 Q-05; Story 1.1 AC3; Story 7.6 · merged as C6
Salla order webhooks carry names, phones, emails and addresses; the attribution store will hold PII by default. Joining a pseudonymous session to an order re-identifies the shopper. "Consent-aware" names no consent source and no default when no signal exists. Q-05 is "pending" while pilots will process real Saudi shopper data. Retention and export/deletion are missing. Nothing covers controller/processor roles, a DPA, breach notification, IPs in logs, or data-subject requests.
Fix: Add a Data & Privacy block: order data minimization, lawful basis and consent defaults, retention periods per data class, merchant export/delete, Slips-as-processor plus DPA, breach runbook. Make Q-05 a blocker for any production or pilot data. Legal sign-off gates Story 7.6.
critical

C7. The injector-key model is incoherent: a public identifier is treated as a secret

FR9; FR17; NFR3; Story 7.1 AC2; Story 5.1 AC1; Story 4.3 AC1; FR2/Story 1.4 AC3 · merged as C7
Any key the storefront script uses is visible in page source to every visitor. Masking and encrypting it is security theater. If it protects the ingestion and content APIs, anyone can copy it and post fake events. "Copy" plus an "installation guide" implies a manual paste, contradicting Story 5.1 and Story 1.5. "Regenerate (old key invalidated)" instantly blanks a hand-installed storefront. After uninstall, "deactivate the injector" can only mean serving a no-op.
Fix: Reclassify the key as a public store identifier; drop "masked/encrypted". Protect the APIs with origin checks, signed short-lived tokens and rate limits. Define one install path from the spike outcome. Regenerate = rotate with an N-hour overlap; uninstall = server returns an empty, non-rendering response.

review-srs-stack-consistency.md
